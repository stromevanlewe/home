# The Battle Inside, and the Freedom Outside It
### Hendrik — Sunday 6 September 2026 | Offence series, message 1

---

## Main Thought

**The war is inside me before it is between us — and the way out is not winning the war but handing myself over to the Spirit, daily, until love becomes the thing I choose by habit.**

The message moves in two steps, and it needs both. The first without the second leaves people staring at their own flesh with no way forward. The second without the first is sentimental.

---

## Movement One — The Battle Is Also Inside

We instinctively put the fight somewhere outside ourselves: spiritual powers in the air, or the person who said the thing. Sometimes it genuinely is. But often the front line runs straight through my own heart and thoughts.

- Jesus won the victory at the cross. That victory is not automatic in my daily reactions — it has to be taken up in the moment I feel the sting.
- The enemy is not the only opponent. My old character is one too.
- The flesh reacts fast. Almost every offence we carry was born in the first ten seconds.
- The first step is not to fight harder. It is to **admit the battle is there.** As long as I excuse myself and place the whole problem outside me, the enemy keeps me tied to it.

## Movement Two — Freedom Is Cultivated, Not Received Passively

Here is where the message actually lands.

**Freedom comes as I bring my flesh, my heart and my mind under the leading of God's Spirit.** Not once, at conversion, but as a way of living. And the shape that submission takes is love — chosen, repeatedly, especially when it is not deserved and not felt. That is the mechanism of transformation: the old self does not argue itself into becoming a new creation, it gets displaced by a thousand chosen acts of love.

Then the corporate effect, which is the point Hendrik was driving at:

**When enough people in a community start walking in that leading, unity is not organised — it happens.** Because a community where people are walking in the Spirit is a community where people feel accepted. And people who feel accepted no longer need to be the best. They no longer need to be right. They no longer need to be seen.

### Main Verse — 2 Corinthians 3:17

> **2 Corinthians 3:17 — NLT**
> For the Lord is the Spirit, and wherever the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom.
>
> **ESV**
> Now the Lord is the Spirit, and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom.

The Spirit's work is not only to convict. It is to form love in you — enough of it that you have some to hand out.

---

## How This Connects to Offence

This is the hinge of the whole series, so it is worth stating plainly.

**Offence needs fuel, and the fuel is my need to be right, to be best, or to be seen.** Remove the need and the offence has very little to burn.

That reframes the standard advice. We usually tell people to forgive faster, or to assume the best, or to go and sort it out. All good. But those are attempts to manage the flame. Hendrik went after the fuel supply.

Three practical consequences:

1. **Saints against saints is not always a demonic attack.** Ephesians 6 is real, but sometimes it is simply two believers with unsurrendered flesh in the same room. Naming that honestly is not defeat — it is the door.
2. **Being slow to react is the Spirit's pace, not passivity.** The flesh's whole strategy depends on speed.
3. **A community that accepts people disarms offence before it starts.** This is why the study is being done *together* and not alone.

### Bridge to Study 1 — The Trap

Sunday's message and Session 1 of the study are the same picture from two ends.

Session 1 says the bite is yours. Sunday said why it happens so fast: the flesh reacts before you do. That is not an excuse, it is a diagnosis. Paul says the war is real precisely because the Spirit is genuinely in you now (Gal 5:17).

And the turtles: some drowned less than a foot from air. The net did not kill them. Not getting out did. Sunday added the other half. Getting out is not the end of it. A turtle in open water is not busy escaping. It is doing what it was made for. Freedom is not the absence of a net. It is a life you cultivate: flesh, heart and mind handed over to the Spirit, love chosen often enough that it becomes the reflex.

Then the part that belongs to a group like ours. When enough people live that way, nobody needs to be right, or best, or seen — and offence runs out of things to snag on.

*Note for the group: this is where the study lands in Session 8. Sunday showed us the destination before we walk the road.*

## Supporting Verses (paraphrased)

**Galatians 5:13–15** — You were called to freedom, but freedom is not a licence for the flesh; serve one another in love instead. Then the warning that lands directly on offence: if you keep biting and tearing at each other, you will destroy each other.

**Galatians 5:16** — Walk by the Spirit and you will not carry out what the flesh craves. The answer to the inner war is not willpower; it is who is leading.

**Romans 12:2** — Do not be shaped by the pattern of this world; be transformed by the renewing of your mind. Transformation happens at the level of how we think, not only how we behave.

**John 16:13** — The Spirit leads us into all truth, and part of that leading is being shown honestly, without condemnation, when we are walking in the old desires.

**Ephesians 4:15–16** — We grow up into Christ by speaking truth in love, each part doing its work. Growth happens *between* us, not only inside us.

**Philippians 2:13** — God is at work in you, giving you both the desire and the power to do what pleases Him. The cultivating is ours; the power is His.

## Application

### From Scripture: Euodia and Syntyche (Philippians 4:2–3)

Two women in the Philippian church, named by name — and every generation since has read that name-check. Paul does not say who was right. He does not say what the disagreement was about. He pleads with them to agree in the Lord, and then turns to the rest of the church and asks them to *help* these two.

- These were not backsliders. Paul says they laboured beside him in the gospel. Serious believers, real gospel work, and still a rift.
- Paul refuses to adjudicate. He does not need to establish who started it — which quietly removes the need to be right.
- The church is asked to carry it with them. Offence between two people is never a private matter inside a body.

Your group has already walked through Philippians, so this will land with recognition.

### From a film: *Remember the Titans*

Not from the pulpit — this one came from watching the film at home the same weekend, and it sits with movement two: the change did not come because somebody finally won the argument. It came because they were in the same room long enough to be known and accepted. Offence rarely survives real proximity.

Worth noticing the second layer too. The boys were already on the team before they went to camp. Selection settled who they belonged to; camp is where the divisions between them actually died.

### From real life: the thing you didn't need to win

Most people in the room can name a moment this month when they replied too fast — a WhatsApp group, a supper table, the church car park. Not a scandal. A sentence.

The useful question is not "was I right?" It is: **what did I need in that moment — to be right, to be best, or to be seen?** Name it, and you have found the fuel line.
